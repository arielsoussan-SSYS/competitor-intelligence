# Competitive sales brief - Aug 25 2026

Formlabs baseline. One page. Read this before any drone or accessible-industrial call.

---

## 1. The one thing to know

**Formlabs ships large-format industrial SLS in Q4 2026 at $84,999, a quarter or two before HP's MJF 1200 arrives in early 2027, and it is already advertising into drone manufacturing by name.**

We have been planning against HP defining the accessible-industrial segment next year. Formlabs is defining it this year.

---

## 2. What you will hear, and what to say

**"The Fuse X1 gives us industrial large-format SLS for under $85,000, and it ships this quarter."**
Price of entry is not cost of ownership. Ask what the qualified material set is on day one: Nylon 12 and Open Material Mode. Nylon 11 is end of 2026. Nylon 12 GF and TPU are announced, not shipping. Then ask what happens when the part needs flame-retardant, ESD-safe or high-temperature material for an airframe. Open Material Mode sounds like freedom. It means the qualification burden moves to the customer.

**"Skyfall, the largest drone manufacturer in Ukraine, prints flight-ready parts the same day they're designed on Formlabs machines."**
Do not dispute it. Reframe. Same-day iteration is a prototyping win and we do it too. Ask what the qualification and traceability package looks like when the part becomes a flight-critical production article on a defense program. Note internally: this claim appears only in outbound copy, never in a published case study. Treat it as **[Unverified]** and do not assert it is false either.

**"A $7,000 printer outperforms one that costs $100,000 a year. There are no tradeoffs."**
The $100,000 comparator is a service bureau contract, not an owned production system. It is a true statement about prototyping economics and a misleading one about production. Move to parts per year, material qualification, repeatability across machines, and what happens at the second and third site.

**"Resin and SLS are production technologies now. Look at 98.6% print success."**
Print success rate is a machine reliability metric, not a part qualification metric. Their proof point is dental aligner moulds: forgiving, single-material, single-geometry. Ask them to show the same numbers on a load-bearing airframe component against a documented material spec.

**From a reseller: "Lightweight drone parts. Printed in-house. Ready in 24 hours."**
24 hours to a part is not 24 hours to a qualified part. This is channel messaging with a "Get a Quote" CTA. Expect it where the buyer is an engineering manager with a departmental budget rather than a program office.

---

## 3. Where Formlabs is vulnerable

- **Materials.** Nylon 12 at launch. Everything else is a roadmap date. Open Material Mode transfers qualification risk to the customer.
- **Ship date.** Q4 2026 has not happened yet. Watch for slip. If it slips, they inherit the same credibility gap we are already using against HP's MJF 1200.
- **Stale creative.** Their heaviest ad, "Turn concepts into reality faster", is 21% of their LinkedIn volume and has been running since at least April 2026. Five months on one message.
- **Their own numbers do not agree.** The Clove Dental ad says 50,000 aligners "a day" in one line and "more than half a million parts a year" in the next. That is off by roughly 30x. If a prospect quotes it, ask which number is right.
- **They are buying prototyping, not production.** 68% of their paid volume is top-funnel prototyping. Production claims only appear in executive posts and outbound, which is where claims get least scrutiny.

---

## 4. Where we are exposed

Say this plainly, because softening it does not help anyone.

- **Drone manufacturing is now a named Formlabs vertical.** Dedicated industry page, published ORQA case study, top-funnel SLS drone creative, and a personalised outbound drone play from a named rep. This is not adjacent to Drone Dominance. It is the same buyer.
- **They are running our argument.** "Bring production in-house instead of outsourcing" is our pitch, made at a fifth of the price.
- **The channel reaches accounts we do not see.** Phillips Machine Tools is running at least four bottom-funnel drone campaigns with quote CTAs. Those leads may never appear in our CRM as competitive.
- **We assumed the wrong competitor.** The accessible-industrial threat is arriving from below, not from HP above.

## 5. Accounts to look at

**ORQA US LLC - Locked.** Formlabs is installed and has published the case study. Fuse 1 and Fuse 1+ 30W, Nylon 12 and TPU 90A, printing end-use parts including frames and propeller guards. They buy powder in 50 kg lots. Do not pitch entry-level SLS here. Pitch what Fuse cannot do: qualified engineering polymers, FDM for large jigs and fixtures, repeatability across a 280,000-unit-a-year line. Their own supply-chain-sovereignty argument is the wedge, because Open Material Mode works against it.

**Skycutter - Contested.** Topped Gauntlet I at 99.3 of 100. Its winning Shrike 10F is built by SkyFall, the manufacturer Formlabs names in outbound. The link is **[Unverified]** and it is the single highest-value thing on this board to verify. Call SkyFall as well as Skycutter.

**Neros - Contested.** Their CEO argues publicly that printing does not solve the supply-chain problem. Read the quote precisely: he is rejecting *distributed printing at the edge*, not additive inside a factory. That is a much narrower claim than it sounds, and it is answerable. Lead with tooling-free bridge production and mid-programme design changes, never with speed-to-part.

**Best timing, all Open:**
- **Ukrainian Defense Drones Tech Corp** - building a greenfield US plant in Holland, Ohio right now. $18.4M committed, 300 jobs pledged. The line is being specified this year.
- **Wilcox Cherry Defense** - new US line standing up in Newington, New Hampshire, aimed at Pentagon orders. 70,000 drones a month across 33 products, which is a part-number problem as much as a volume one.
- **Perennial Autonomy** - $500M IDIQ awarded May 2026, Eric Schmidt-backed. Best-funded name on the board and the ramp has not been built yet.
- **Swarm Defense** - scaling 72,000 to 250,000 units a year, and they missed a Gauntlet I delivery order. Something to prove, and a tooling decision due.
- **Griffon Aerospace** - founder-owned since 1995, still CEO and CTO. Shortest decision chain on the list, and Huntsville is an easy visit.
